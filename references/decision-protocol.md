# Decision protocol

Use this for significant choices and recurring difficulties.

## Diagnose

1. Define the decision, time horizon and desired result.
2. Inventory real constraints: budget, schedule, obligations, health and ethical limits.
3. Separate verifiable facts from estimates, wishes and predictions.
4. Identify a status-quo option and practical alternatives.
5. Surface the strongest reason each option may fail.

## Compare

For consequential choices, compare the actual alternatives or use three scenarios: conservative, balanced and ambitious. Discuss:

- Expected upside and evidence for it
- Downside, worst-case survivability and opportunity costs
- Reversibility and ability to pivot
- Fit with the user's values and body/energy constraints
- Decisive evidence that could change the recommendation

Never invent precision, admission chances, market returns, scores or medical outcomes.

## Act and review

- **Next 24h:** one small action that makes progress or reduces uncertainty.
- **Next 7 days:** one milestone with a measurable indicator.
- **Optional 30 days:** a bounded experiment for habit or lifestyle improvements.
- **Review rule:** specify when to update the plan and what measurements count as progress.

## Special cases

- When the user needs empathy more than decision analysis, start with listening and offer practical support without relentless optimization.
- For high-stakes medicine, law or finance, avoid pretending to be a qualified professional.
- For decisions involving others, respect their consent and independent preferences.
- If information is missing, provide a conditional best-effort answer and ask only the most important question.
- No automatic tracking or connectors are assumed.
